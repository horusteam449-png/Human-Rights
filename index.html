Introduction to Human Rights

Universal

Human rights apply to all humans—everywhere, regardless of nationality, gender, race, or religion.

Inalienable

No one can take your rights away unless under specific lawful circumstances.

Interconnected

All rights support each other—freedom, justice, and equality work together.

Cyrus Cylinder

The Cyrus Cylinder (539 BC) is considered one of the earliest declarations of human rights. It promoted freedom of religion, protection of cultures, ending forced labor, and allowing displaced people to return to their homeland.

Magna Carta

Signed in 1215, the Magna Carta limited the king’s power, established fair trials, and stated that no one is above the law. It influenced many modern constitutions.

Petition of Rights

The Petition of Right (1628) stated that taxes required Parliament's approval, protected people from unlawful imprisonment, and limited military power over civilians.

Historical Evolution Timeline

539 BC – Cyrus Cylinder

The world's first known declaration of human rights issued by Cyrus the Great in Babylon.

1215 – Magna Carta

A cornerstone in limiting absolute monarchy and establishing rule of law.

1628 – Petition of Rights

Formal statement of civil liberties and government limitations in England.

1948 – Universal Declaration of Human Rights

The most important global human rights document ever created by the UN.
